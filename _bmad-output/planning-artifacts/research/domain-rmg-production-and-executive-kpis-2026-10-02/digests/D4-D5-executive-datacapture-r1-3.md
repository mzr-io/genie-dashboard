## 2. KPI entries

Reading the blocks:
- **"30 s?"** asks whether a ~30-second refresh is realistic.
- **"Natural cadence"** is the grain at which the KPI is meaningful.
- Unless stated otherwise, the refresh and cadence judgements are reasoned inferences from the data-capture evidence in rows #22–#26. They are not quoted claims.

### K1. On-Time Delivery (OTD)
- **Aliases:** on-time shipment, OTD %, delivery performance.
- **Definition and variants:**
  - (a) Orders shipped on time ÷ orders shipped in the month. Counted by order; the reference date is not specified [#3].
  - (b) Measured against the buyer's requested date [#16].
  - (c) Measured against the supplier-confirmed date [#16].
  - (d) For the factory, "on time" in practice means meeting the ex-factory date, which may differ from the shipment or arrival date [#15].
  - (e) Retailer variant: Walmart splits on-time by freight terms. "Collect-Ready" means ready at pickup; "Prepaid" means arrival on time [#19].
  - The counting unit also varies: by order [#3], by PO line [#19], or by cases [#19].
- **Source data:** PO/order ID, buyer, the reference date (ex-factory, buyer-requested, confirmed, or ETD/ETA), actual ex-factory or handover date, shipped quantity, any agreed tolerance window.
- **Granularity:** PO or PO-line events, rolled up by buyer, factory and company, by week or month.
- **30 s?** No, not in a meaningful way. The KPI changes only when a shipment event is posted, a few times a day at most. A live "shipments due today or this week and their status" list can refresh often, but the ratio itself is an event-driven roll-up.
- **Natural cadence:** monthly [#3], with weekly views; brands score it per season or period (unverified belief for the season cadence).
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated from stored shipment events.
- **Module:** shipment/commercial, plus orders/merchandising.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** one agreed reference date per buyer; a frozen original date versus revised dates (buyer-approved extensions); partial shipments handled consistently.
- **Decision supported:** buyer relationship risk, discount and air-freight exposure [#20], and which lines or orders to expedite.

### K2. On-Time In-Full (OTIF)
- **Definition:** on time AND the full quantity [#17].
- **Variants:** Walmart's thresholds and its 3% COGS fine at PO-line level [#19] (retail, not apparel-specific). In apparel, "in full" interacts with the buyer's allowed short or excess shipment tolerance. That tolerance practice is unverified and not evidenced here.
- **Source data:** as K1, plus ordered and shipped quantity per PO line or size/colour.
- **Granularity:** PO line; rolled up by buyer and month.
- **30 s?** No (event-driven).
- **Natural cadence:** monthly or per buyer reporting period.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** shipment/commercial, orders.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a PO-line master with size/colour breakdown; the buyer's tolerance rules stored per buyer.
- **Decision supported:** chargeback and fine exposure, and buyer scorecard standing.

### K3. Cut-to-Ship Ratio
- **Variants (they conflict; see §4):**
  - (a) Cut qty ÷ Shipped qty, target 1, typical value about 1.02 [#1, #5].
  - (b) Shipped ÷ Cut × 100, about 98% called "very good" [#7, #8].
- **Source data:** cut quantity per order (cutting reports or bundle generation) and shipped quantity per order (commercial invoice or packing list).
- **Granularity:** order/PO or style-colour; computed once the order is closed.
- **30 s?** No. It is only meaningful once the order has shipped (a closed order). A running "cut vs shipped to date" can be shown, but it is a WIP indicator, not the KPI.
- **Natural cadence:** per order at closure, reported monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** production (cutting), shipment.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** cut quantity recorded per order (re-cuts and replacement panels included or excluded consistently); shipped quantity reconciled to the invoice.
- **Decision supported:** control of extra-cut allowance (2–5% [#6]), leftover stock and rejects.

### K4. Order-to-Ship Ratio (also called ship-to-order)
- **Variants:**
  - (a) Order qty ÷ Shipped qty, target 1 [#2].
  - (b) The inverse, shipped ÷ ordered, is the natural form of the "in-full" component in K2. That inverse is an inference, not a cited formula.
- **Source data:** ordered and shipped quantity per PO.
- **Granularity:** PO, by month.
- **30 s?** No. **Natural cadence:** per order closure, monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** orders, shipment.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** amended PO quantities versioned.
- **Decision supported:** short- and excess-shipment control.

### K5. Order lead time
- **Definition:** order placement to shipment of the completed order [#13].
- **Variants:** buyer-side and vendor-blog lead times quoted in business days [#31]. Better Buying rates the fairness of buyer lead times [#30].
- **Source data:** PO receipt or confirmation date, ex-factory or shipment date.
- **Granularity:** PO, by buyer, product type and month.
- **30 s?** No. **Natural cadence:** per order, monthly or seasonal trend.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** orders/merchandising, shipment.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** which "start" date is used (PO date, confirmation, or approval of PP sample or fabric) must be fixed. The sources do not agree on a start event.
- **Decision supported:** quoting and capacity acceptance, and negotiation with buyers.

### K6. Marker efficiency
- **Variants:**
  - (a) Area basis: used marker area ÷ total marker area × 100 [#9].
  - (b) Weight basis [#14].
  - Benchmark: above 80–85% is good [#14] (low confidence).
- **Source data:** CAD marker outputs (pattern area, marker length and width).
- **Granularity:** marker, style/size ratio; event.
- **30 s?** It is available as soon as a marker is made, but markers are created a few times per order, so a 30 s refresh adds nothing.
- **Natural cadence:** per marker or order; reported weekly or monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** stored (from CAD).
- **Module:** production (cutting/CAD).
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a CAD integration or manual capture of marker records.
- **Decision supported:** fabric cost control.

### K7. Fabric utilisation and consumption variance
- **Variants:**
  - Fabric utilisation = fabric used in garments ÷ fabric available × 100 [#10].
  - Marked consumption versus achieved consumption [#11]. Achieved consumption = fabric bought ÷ garments shipped. The variance is achieved minus marked, or achieved minus costed/booked. The "costed/booked" comparison is an inference.
- **Source data:** fabric received and issued per order (stores), lay or spreading records, cut quantity, shipped quantity, CAD consumption, costing consumption.
- **Granularity:** order/style; closed-order basis.
- **30 s?** No. **Natural cadence:** per order at closure, monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** inventory/stores, production (cutting), orders (costing).
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** fabric issues booked to the right order; returns and leftovers recorded.
- **Decision supported:** costing accuracy for future quotes, and wastage.

### K8. Capacity utilisation and capacity booking (order book)
- **Definition:** produced minutes ÷ capacity minutes × 100 [#12]. For order book or booking, the analogous measure is booked SAM-minutes ÷ available minutes per future period. That is an inference; no formula was retrieved.
- **Source data:** order SAM × quantity, line or factory available minutes (operators × hours), planned and produced minutes.
- **Granularity:** factory or line, by week or month (future periods for booking).
- **30 s?** Utilisation of produced minutes could be computed intraday if output is scanned (see D5), but the KPI is a period measure. Booking changes only with order entry. Neither suits 30 s.
- **Natural cadence:** weekly or monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** planning, orders.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** accurate SMV/SAM per style, which is a known weakness (§7); attendance-based available minutes.
- **Decision supported:** accepting or declining orders, subcontracting, overtime.

### K9. Revenue / FOB value shipped
- **Definition:** sum of shipped quantity × FOB unit price per period. No retrieved source gives a formula; the vendor GoTEE advertises "production values" and buyer-wise views [#26].
- **Source data:** invoice or packing list quantity, FOB price per PO line, currency.
- **Granularity:** shipment event, then buyer and month.
- **30 s?** No. **Natural cadence:** daily accumulation, reported monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** shipment/commercial, finance.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** PO price versioning; discounts and credit notes netted out [#20].
- **Decision supported:** cash flow and buyer mix. Related variant: "produced value" = output × FOB or CM per piece. GoTEE implies this from production data, so it could update intraday if output is scanned.

### K10. Cost per minute (CPM) and CM
- **Variants:**
  - CM = SMV × CPM.
  - Labour CPM = salaries ÷ SAM produced, OR salaries ÷ available minutes.
  - Factory CPM = total factory cost ÷ available minutes.
  - CM adjusted by efficiency = SAM × CPM ÷ efficiency [#21].
- **Source data:** payroll, overheads, available and produced minutes, SAM per style.
- **Granularity:** factory or line, by month.
- **30 s?** No. Payroll and overhead are monthly.
- **Natural cadence:** monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** finance, workforce, production.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** SMV accuracy; payroll by line.
- **Decision supported:** quoting floor price, and margin per order or buyer.

### K11. Air-freight and late-shipment cost (discounts, chargebacks)
- **Definition:** cost of air shipment the factory bears, plus discounts or claims on export receipts for late shipment [#20]. Retail fines can be 3% of COGS [#19] (Walmart, not apparel-specific).
- **Source data:** freight invoices flagged as factory-paid air, debit or credit notes, discounts on bills.
- **Granularity:** shipment or PO, by buyer and month.
- **30 s?** No. **Natural cadence:** monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** stored per event; aggregated.
- **Module:** shipment/commercial, finance.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a root-cause tag (material delay, production, buyer change).
- **Decision supported:** the cost of lateness, and holding suppliers or buyers accountable.

### K12. Shipment status (pipeline)
- **Definition:** the count or quantity of POs by stage (in production, ready, ex-factory done, shipped), set against ex-factory date [#15]. No formula was retrieved; this is a status list, not a ratio.
- **Granularity:** PO.
- **30 s?** The refresh mechanism is trivial. Underlying stages update when people post events (packing, inspection, handover), so a frequent refresh shows at most hourly changes. If packing or finishing output is scanned, the "ready quantity" can move near real-time; that is an inference.
- **Natural cadence:** daily.
- **Module:** shipment, production, quality (final inspection).
- **Decision supported:** daily expediting.

### K13. Buyer scorecard composite
- **Components:** quality, OTD, responsiveness, order accuracy, price stability, compliance, capacity [#18]. Walmart-style OTIF components [#19].
- **Granularity:** buyer, by period.
- **30 s?** No. **Natural cadence:** monthly or seasonal (cadence is an unverified belief).
- **Stored or calculated:** brands compute and send these. Whether a factory receives the brand's own scorecard numbers was not evidenced.
- **Module:** shipment, quality, orders.
- **Decision supported:** buyer retention and allocation.

### K14 (D5 context). Hourly line output and line efficiency, as the near-real-time contrast case
- **Definition:** efficiency = minutes produced × 100 ÷ minutes attended [#4].
- **Source data:** pieces per operation or end-of-line, style SMV, operators present × time.
- **30 s?** Realistic only where output is captured automatically, by per-bundle RFID scans [#22, #26] or machine sensors [#24]. With manual hourly boards the effective refresh is hourly. The manual-board latency is an unverified belief (see §7).
- **Natural cadence:** hourly or shift.
- **Module:** production, workforce.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** SMV accuracy; attendance timing (GoTEE explicitly reconciles workstation and gate attendance [#26], which signals a known gap).

**Implication (inference from the evidence above).**
- A ~30 s refresh is honest only for sewing-floor flow metrics (output, WIP, efficiency, idle time, machine faults), and only in plants with per-bundle scanning or machine sensors.

- All D4 executive KPIs (OTD, OTIF, cut-to-ship, order-to-ship, lead time, fabric utilisation, consumption variance, FOB shipped, CPM/CM, air-freight cost, scorecards) are event-driven roll-ups. Their natural cadence is daily to monthly whatever the capture technology.

## 4. Formula and terminology variations

1. **Cut-to-ship direction.**
   - OCS: cut ÷ ship, target 1, typical about 1.02 [#1, #5].
   - Apparel Resources: ship ÷ cut × 100, about 98% "very good" [#7, #8].
   - These are reciprocal, so the same order reads either 1.02 or 98%. Both are in active use; the label alone does not tell you which one is meant.
2. **Order-to-ship direction.** OCS uses order ÷ ship [#2]. "Ship-to-order" (shipped ÷ ordered) is the implied inverse. No retrieved source defines the inverse explicitly.
3. **OTD reference date.**
   - Buyer-requested date versus supplier-confirmed date [#16].
   - Ex-factory date as the factory's own "shipment date", buffered before the buyer's delivery date [#15].
   - Retailer split: collect-ready versus prepaid on-time [#19].
4. **OTD counting unit.** Orders per month [#3], PO lines [#19], or cases [#19].
5. **Fabric consumption.** Marked consumption (CAD ÷ cut) versus achieved consumption (bought ÷ shipped) [#11]. These differ by both the numerator and the denominator.
6. **Marker efficiency.** Area basis [#9] versus weight basis [#14].
7. **Cost per minute.** Labour-only versus whole-factory. The labour denominator is either SAM produced or available minutes. Some sources apply an efficiency divisor and some do not [#21].
8. **Lead time start event.** Order placement [#13]. Other start events (PO confirmation, approvals) were not evidenced.

## 5. Glossary

Citations are given where the evidence exists. Terms marked (UB) are unverified beliefs: standard trade usage that this session could not source.

- **Buyer:** the brand or retailer, or its buying house, that places orders. Buying houses buffer between ex-factory and delivery dates [#15].
- **Merchandiser** (UB): the factory-side owner of an order, from costing, sampling and TNA through to shipment.
- **Style / colourway / PO** (UB): a style is a garment design; a colourway is a colour version of it; a PO (purchase order) is the buyer's order, broken down by style, colour, size, quantity, price and delivery date.
- **FOB (Free On Board)** (UB): price and terms where the seller's cost and risk end when goods are loaded at the origin port. In RMG usage it also means the full garment price including materials, as opposed to CM.
- **CM (Cost of Making / Cut-Make)**: the conversion cost per garment. CM = SMV × CPM [#21].
- **CMT (Cut-Make-Trim)** (UB): a business model in which the buyer supplies materials and the factory charges for conversion only.
- **CPM (Cost per minute)**: factory or labour cost per available or produced minute [#21].
- **SMV / SAM**: standard minute value or standard allowed minutes per garment or operation, used in efficiency and CM [#4, #21].
- **Ex-factory date**: the date the shipment must leave the factory for the forwarder [#15].
- **ETD / ETA** (UB): estimated time of departure or arrival of the vessel or flight.
- **PCD (Planned Cut Date)** (UB): the date cutting must begin for an order to meet ex-factory.
- **TNA (Time and Action calendar)** (UB): the order critical path, setting dates for approvals, material in-house, PCD, inspection and ex-factory.
- **PP sample (pre-production sample)** (UB): the sample approved by the buyer before bulk production.
- **Size set** (UB): samples in each size made before bulk to check grading and fit.
- **Extra cutting %**: the cut allowance above order quantity, typically 2–5% [#5, #6].
- **Cut-to-ship / order-to-ship**: see K3, K4 and §4.
- **OTD / OTIF**: see K1, K2. **Collect vs prepaid**: freight-term split that decides who owns transit [#19].
- **Consolidation** (UB): combining several POs or suppliers into one container or shipment at a consolidator's warehouse.
- **Air shipment**: shipping by air, at the factory's cost, to recover lateness [#20].
- **Discount / claim / chargeback**: a deduction on export receipts for late or non-compliant shipments [#20]. Retail OTIF fines run at 3% of COGS [#19].
- **Marker / marker efficiency / fabric utilisation / marked vs achieved consumption**: see K6, K7 [#9–#11].
- **Capacity utilisation**: produced minutes ÷ capacity minutes [#12].
- **Plan Performance Index**: plan versus actual adherence [#13].
- **DHU / RFT**: defects per hundred units; right first time [#4].
- **MMR**: man-to-machine ratio [#4].
- **Bundle ticket / RFID bundle tag**: the identifier for a bundle of cut pieces, scanned at each operation [#22, #26].
- **Shop-floor control system (SFCS)**: an RFID or barcode real-time production tracking system [#22].
