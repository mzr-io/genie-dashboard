## 2. KPI entries

Notes that apply to every entry:
- **Refresh feasibility.** A ~30-second refresh is only meaningful when the inputs are captured digitally at the event: tablet or RFID per piece or bundle, IoT on machines, biometric attendance. When the data comes from hourly paper boards or end-of-day keying, refresh is bounded by the entry cadence no matter how often the dashboard polls. This is my reasoning from the capture methods described in #29 and #11, not a sourced claim.
- **Module labels** are my inference, not sourced.

### D2: Quality

#### Q1. DHU (Defects per Hundred Units)
- **Aliases:** DHU%, defects per hundred units, defect rate (ambiguous; see Q2).
- **Definition and formula:** DHU = total defects found / total pieces checked × 100 [#1, #3]. It counts defects, so one garment can add several, and DHU can exceed 100.
- **Raw fields:** pieces checked and defect count, each with checkpoint (inline / end-line / final), line, style/order, operation and defect code when available, checker ID, timestamp.
- **Granularity:** checkpoint × line × hour is the normal floor view. It rolls up to floor and factory. Operator-level DHU needs the operation that caused each defect recorded.
- **~30 s refresh:** realistic only with tablet or digital end-line checking that records each piece in real time. With hourly paper tally sheets, the true cadence is hourly.
- **Natural period:** hourly on the floor; shift and day for management, with trend by week and month.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated from stored checked and defect counts.
- **Module:** quality, plus production (line/style).
- **Data-quality prerequisites:**
  - a consistent defect-code master;
  - a clear rule that every piece checked is counted, not only the defective ones;
  - each checkpoint kept separate, so pieces are not double-counted across stages;
  - "defect" and "defective" not mixed up.
- **Decision supported:** line and checker quality focus, rework-capacity planning (repair labour scales with DHU [#5]), supplier or buyer quality escalation. Benchmark bands exist only from a vendor [#4] (L).

#### Q2. Defective % (percentage defective) vs defect count
- **Aliases:** defective rate, % defectives, sometimes loosely called "defect %".
- **Formula:** defective pieces / pieces checked × 100 [#2]. The value is bounded at 100%. In the same sample it is always ≤ DHU (e.g. DHU 30 vs 7.5% defective [#2]).
- **Raw fields:** pieces checked and pieces with ≥1 defect, at the same granularity as Q1.
- **Granularity, refresh, period, stored/calculated, module:** as for Q1.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** the checker must record each defective piece, not only defect tallies. Many paper tally sheets record defects only, so defective % cannot be derived from them.
- **Decision supported:** share of output that needs repair, which drives rework and alteration-table capacity.

#### Q3. Rejection % (reject vs alter/rework)
- **Aliases:** reject rate, rejection rate, cut-to-ship loss (partly overlapping).
- **Definitions:**
  - *Reject* = a garment that cannot be repaired by any means [#8].
  - *Alter/rework* = a defective garment that is repaired and returned to flow. Implied by #8 and #7; no source fetched gives a formal alteration-rate formula.
- **Formula variants:**
  - (a) Order-level: rejected garments / total cut quantity × 100, which can be proxied by cut qty − shipped qty [#8].
  - (b) Checkpoint-level: rejects / pieces checked at a checkpoint. This is common practice but **unverified belief**: no source fetched states it.
  - Variant (a) also counts shortages and losses that are not quality rejects. Report the two separately.
- **Raw fields:** cut quantity per order, rejects by stage and reason (fabric/material vs process), shipped quantity.
- **Granularity:** order/style for variant (a); stage × line × day for variant (b).
- **~30 s refresh:** not meaningful for variant (a), which closes at shipment. Variant (b) can refresh near real-time if rejects are scanned.
- **Natural period:** per order (closing) and monthly; daily for floor control.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** quality, production (cutting), shipment, orders.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** cut quantity reconciled per order; reject reason coded as material vs process. One snippet says over 50% of rejections are material-caused [#9] (L).
- **Decision supported:** order-margin leakage, material-supplier claims, cut-plan allowance (extra cut %).

#### Q4. RFT (Right First Time) / first-pass yield
- **Aliases:** RFT %, FTT (first time through), FPY, first-time-right.
- **Formula:** first-time-passed units / total production units × 100 [#6].
- **How it differs:** lot pass % is not RFT, because passed lots can include repaired pieces [#7]. RFT is the complement of defective % only when it is measured at the same checkpoint over the same population (my inference).
- **Raw fields:** units inspected, and per unit whether it passed on first presentation; requires tracking at piece level or as a first-pass count.
- **Granularity:** line × checkpoint × hour or day.
- **~30 s refresh:** needs digital piece-level pass/fail at end-line. With hourly counts it can only be hourly.
- **Natural period:** hourly or shift on the floor; daily and weekly for management.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** quality.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a re-presented (repaired) piece must not be counted again as a first-time pass. This requires piece or bundle identity.
- **Decision supported:** process capability per line; incentive schemes; comparing lines regardless of repair capacity.

#### Q5. Inline / end-of-line / final inspection results; traffic-light status
- **Definitions:**
  - Inline inspection checks semi-stitched work during the process, at fixed points or by roving checkers, on 100% or a sample of pieces [#10].
  - End-of-line inspection is referenced in #10 but not formally defined in any source fetched.
  - Final inspection is the AQL-based audit; see Q6.
- **Traffic-light metric:** per operator, sample 10 pieces every 1–2 h. Red if ≥5 defective, yellow if ≥2, green if 0 [#11]. Thresholds are SOP-specific (L).
- **Derived KPIs:** % operators in red/yellow per round; DHU by checkpoint (Q1).
- **Raw fields:** round timestamp, operator, operation, sample size, defectives, colour.
- **Granularity:** operator/workstation × round (1–2 h).
- **~30 s refresh:** the data itself only changes every 1–2 h per operator, so 30 s polling adds no information. Floor-wide status changes as rounds complete.
- **Natural period:** round (hourly); summarised by shift.
- **Stored or calculated:** colour is a stored event; % red is calculated.
- **Module:** quality, plus workforce (operator master).
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** operator-to-operation mapping kept current; round adherence logged.
- **Decision supported:** where the line supervisor and quality staff intervene; operator retraining.

#### Q6. Final audit (AQL) pass/fail rate
- **Aliases:** final inspection pass rate, FRI pass %, audit acceptance rate.
- **Definition:** lots are sampled under ISO 2859-1. Example: lot 1,000, General Inspection Level II → sample 80, AQL 2.5 → accept ≤5, reject ≥6 major [#13]. Common apparel defaults are AQL 2.5 for major and 4.0 for minor defects [#13] (M; the ISO standard was not read directly).
- **Formula:** lots passed / lots inspected × 100. This is **unverified belief** (standard usage; no fetched source states it). An alternative weighting by quantity (pcs in passed lots / pcs offered) is equally plausible and also unverified.
- **Raw fields:** lot/PO, lot size, inspection level, AQL, sample size, major/minor counts, result, inspector (internal or buyer/3rd-party), date.
- **Granularity:** lot/inspection event, rolled up by order, buyer, factory, week/month.
- **~30 s refresh:** not meaningful, because there are only a few inspection events per day.
- **Natural period:** event-based; reviewed weekly or monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** the result is stored; the rate is calculated.
- **Module:** quality, plus orders and shipment.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a re-inspection kept distinct from a first inspection, with first-attempt pass rate reported separately.
- **Decision supported:** shipment risk, buyer relationship, and air-freight/penalty exposure.

#### Q7. Buyer inspection failure rate
- **Definition:** the share of buyer or third-party final inspections that fail. The same mechanics as Q6, with the inspector type set to buyer/3rd-party.
- **Evidence:** no source fetched defines this as a separate KPI. It is derived from Q6 (**unverified belief** as a named KPI).
- **Other fields, module and decision:** as Q6.

#### Q8. Top defect types (Pareto)
- **Definition:** defect counts by defect code, sorted with a cumulative %.
- **Evidence:** no source fetched specifically; standard quality-tool usage (**unverified belief**).
- **Raw fields:** defect code, operation, line, checkpoint, count.
- **Granularity:** line/checkpoint × day or week.
- **~30 s refresh:** feasible with digital defect capture, but the ranking only stabilises over hours.
- **Natural period:** daily and weekly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** quality.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a standard defect library across checkers.
- **Decision supported:** where to spend kaizen and training effort.

#### Q9. Cutting and finishing quality
- **Sourced measures:**
  - cutting-stage rejects, which feed rejection % [#8];
  - cutting, finishing and washing WIP reconciliation [#25, #26].
- **Unsourced measures:** cutting-specific KPIs (e.g. cut-panel defects, marker efficiency, shade/size mix-ups) and finishing DHU were not found in fetched sources and remain **unverified**.

### D3: Workforce, WIP and flow

#### W1. Absenteeism %
- **Formula variants (do not merge):**
  - (a) **ILO:** absenteeism = unplanned leave, *including* sick leave and *excluding* holiday leave [#16]. Reported in days lost per year in that study [#17].
  - (b) **Generic manufacturing (vendor):** absent days / available working days × 100, where approved paid leave is taken out of the available days [#18] (L).
  - (c) **Daily headcount:** number absent today / total staff employed × 100 [#19] (L).
- **Where the variants differ:** whether sick leave counts (ILO says yes); whether approved or planned leave is excluded from the denominator, the numerator, or both; and whether the measure is person-days or headcount.
- **Raw fields:** employee ID, roster/scheduled shift, attendance status per day (present / absent with leave type: sick, casual, earned, maternity, unauthorised), department/line, grade (operator vs staff).
- **Granularity:** worker × day, rolled up to line, floor and factory.
- **~30 s refresh:** the morning headcount figure is effectively fixed once punch-in closes. Biometric or RFID punch-in gives near real-time data during the first hour; after that, little changes.
- **Natural period:** daily (morning); monthly for HR.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated from stored attendance.
- **Module:** workforce/HR/attendance.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** leave-type coding, a roster of who is expected, and handling of late arrivals and half-days.
- **Decision supported:** daily line manning and re-allocation. ILO notes that whole lines could not run and shipments were cancelled [#17]. Also welfare and retention programmes.

#### W2. Labour turnover %
- **Formula variants:**
  - (a) **OCS (garment):** leavers in 12 months / monthly average employment × 100. Production workers only; an operator-only sub-variant exists [#14].
  - (b) **Generic HR:** departures / average employees × 100, covering all staff [#15].
- **Differences between variants:**
  - population covered (workers only vs all staff);
  - period (annual vs monthly);
  - whether dismissals and transfers count. OCS says "left or terminated" [#14].
- **Benchmark:** ILO Myanmar reports 54.2% annual turnover across 14 factories [#17] (H, but for one country).
- **Raw fields:** joining and leaving dates, separation type, category, headcount at month-end or average.
- **Granularity:** factory/floor/department × month.
- **~30 s refresh:** not meaningful.
- **Natural period:** monthly and annual.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** HR.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a consistent separation date and reason, and no re-hire double counting.
- **Decision supported:** recruitment and training budget, retention strategy, skill-matrix risk.

#### W3. Operator utilisation
- **Evidence:** no fetched source gives a garment-specific formula (**unverified**).
- **Candidate definition (unverified belief):** (available minutes − NPT/lost minutes) / available minutes. This links directly to NPT (W8). Line efficiency (#22) is a related but different measure, because it includes performance against SMV.
- **Module:** IE, production.

#### W4. Manpower vs machine allocation
- **Evidence:** no fetched source defines a KPI. Kong et al. use station count and fixed seats in the hanger line (S ≤ 32) as a constraint in balancing [#23].
- **Candidate measures (unverified belief):** operators present / machines (or workstations) planned per line; machine idle count.
- **Module:** IE, production, maintenance.

#### W5. WIP per line and WIP days
- **Formulas:**
  - Piece-count WIP: cutting WIP = cut − sent to sewing; sewing line WIP = loaded − completed; finishing WIP = received − packed [#25, #26]. Washing WIP = sent − received [#26].
  - Critical WIP = bottleneck rate × throughput time [#27] (L).
- **Benchmark:** about 1 h of WIP per operation (30–120 min range) [#28] (L).
- **WIP days:** WIP / average daily output is **not found** in fetched sources (unverified, though Little's law [#27] implies it).
- **Raw fields:** cumulative input and output per section/line per style, plus bundle movements if bundle-tracked.
- **Granularity:** section/line × style, as a running snapshot. Operation-level WIP needs bundle or piece scanning.
- **~30 s refresh:** realistic for line-level WIP only if input and output are scanned (RFID/barcode per bundle). With hourly boards, WIP updates hourly.
- **Natural period:** a snapshot; reviewed daily.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated from input/output counters.
- **Module:** production, cutting, finishing, planning.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** input loading to the line recorded, not just output; rejects and outsourced pieces (washing, embroidery) accounted for.
- **Decision supported:** cutting issue release, line loading, flow problems, and cash tied in WIP.

#### W6. Line balancing ratio / line balance efficiency
- **Formula variants:**
  - (a) **LBR** = bottleneck (lowest) capacity per hour × garment SMV / (line manpower × 60) × 100 [#20]. Example 63.75%.
  - (b) Balance efficiency = total work content / (no. of operators × cycle time) × 100 [#21] (L). This is the classic ALB form, using the longest station time as the cycle time.
  - (c) Line efficiency (output × SMV / (manpower × working minutes)) is sometimes conflated with balance efficiency [#22]. It is a different KPI, because it uses actual output.
  - Kong et al. frame balancing by a minimum cycle time and measure the outcome in UPPH [#23].
- **Raw fields:**
  - operation bulletin (operations, SMV);
  - operator-to-operation allocation;
  - per-operation capacity from time study (pcs/h) or from actual hourly output per operator.
- **Granularity:** line × style changeover / day; inputs at operation-operator level.
- **~30 s refresh:**
  - Design-time LBR is static per style.
  - "Live" LBR needs per-operator output capture (e.g. bundle-ticket scan or hanger-system counts) and would still be noisy at 30 s. Hourly is the realistic minimum (my reasoning).
- **Natural period:** per style setup, re-checked daily or hourly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** IE/engineering, production.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** an accurate operation bulletin/SMV and a current operator allocation.
- **Decision supported:** operator reallocation, extra machines at bottleneck, style feasibility.

#### W7. Throughput time and bottleneck operation
- **Bottleneck:** the operation where work piles up because of low capacity or performance [#24]. The upstream rate should equal or slightly exceed the downstream rate [#23].
- **Throughput time:** the relation Wc = Rb × To [#27] (L). A direct garment definition (time from line input to line output) was not fetched (**unverified**).
- **Raw fields:** per-operation capacity/output; timestamps of bundle in and out.
- **~30 s refresh:** needs bundle/piece scan at line in and out (throughput time) or per-operation counts (bottleneck).
- **Natural period:** hourly/daily.
- **Module:** IE, production.
- **Decision supported:** where to add an operator or machine; lead-time promises.

#### W8. Lost time / NPT and downtime by reason
- **Definition:** NPT ("loss time") is time lost while operators are present. Reasons include waiting for work, machine breakdown, power failure, repair work and unexplained idle time [#29]. Causes also include layout, balancing, planning, shared tools, absence and fatigue [#30].
- **Formula:** no explicit formula in fetched sources. NPT % = lost minutes / available minutes is an **unverified belief**.
- **Capture:** operators entered reasons into a real-time tracking device [#29].
- **Raw fields:** start/end or duration, reason code, operator/machine, line.
- **Granularity:** operator/machine × event, rolled up to line, shift and day.
- **~30 s refresh:**
  - realistic if events are captured by device or IoT, e.g. machine-on sensors;
  - not realistic if NPT is reconstructed at end of shift by supervisors.
- **Natural period:** shift/day; Pareto by reason weekly.
- **Stored or calculated:** stored events; NPT % calculated.
- **Module:** production, maintenance, IE.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** a standard reason-code list and no unexplained gaps.
- **Decision supported:** maintenance priority, feeding/planning fixes. Case gains of 6–10% [#30] and >10% [#29].

#### W9. Overtime %
- **Evidence:** no fetched source gives a formula (**unverified**).
- **Candidate formula (unverified belief):** OT hours / regular hours × 100, by worker/line/month.
- **Related evidence:** ILO notes that overtime to recover absence-driven losses is a hidden cost [#17].
- **Module:** HR/attendance, payroll.
- **Decision supported:** compliance (buyer codes, labour law), and cost.

#### W10. Cost per minute (CPM) / cost per piece
- **Formula variants:**
  - (a) **Produced minutes:** CPM = (manpower + overhead cost) / (OK pcs × SMV). Recommended daily [#31].
  - (b) **Available minutes:** labour CPM = salary / available minutes at 100% efficiency; factory CPM = annual operating cost / available working minutes [#32].
  - Cost per garment = SAM × CPM / efficiency [#32].
- **How the variants differ:** variant (a) rises when efficiency falls, which makes it a health indicator [#31]. Variant (b) is a costing/quotation rate that is independent of performance, and is also used for living-wage costing [#33].
- **Raw fields:** payroll cost (daily accrual), overhead allocation, OK output pcs, SMV per style.
- **Granularity:** factory/floor/line × day/month.
- **~30 s refresh:** not realistic. Costs are accrued daily at best, and overhead is usually monthly.
- **Natural period:** daily (per OCS) to monthly.
- **Stored or calculated:** calculated.
- **Module:** finance/costing, production, IE, HR/payroll.
- **Data-quality prerequisites:** an accepted overhead allocation key; SMV per style.
- **Decision supported:** pricing/quote floor, factory health, CXO-level productivity-cost view.

## 4. Glossary

| Term | Definition (with source) |
|---|---|
| **Defect** | A non-conformance not acceptable to the end customer, e.g. broken stitch, hole, broken button [#1, #2]. |
| **Defective (piece)** | A garment with ≥1 defect [#2]. |
| **DHU** | Defects per hundred units = defects / checked × 100 [#1, #3]. |
| **Reject** | A garment that cannot be repaired by any means and is not shipped [#8]. |
| **Alteration / rework / repair** | A defective garment corrected and returned to flow. Contrasted with reject in #8 and #7, but no fetched source gives a formal definition. "Alteration" is also the term used in traffic-light outcome studies [#11]. |
| **RFT / FTT / first-pass yield** | Units passing first time without any correction / total produced [#6]. |
| **AQL** | Acceptable Quality Limit. Under ISO 2859-1, the sample size and accept/reject numbers per lot follow from it. Common apparel values are 2.5 for major and 4.0 for minor defects [#13]. |
| **General Inspection Level II** | Default ISO 2859-1 inspection level used to set sample size [#13]. |
| **Major / minor defect** | Major: likely to cause the consumer to return the item or make it unfit for purpose [#13 snippet]. Minor: below that threshold (unverified). |
| **Inline inspection** | In-process checking of semi-stitched garments, at fixed points or by roving checkers [#10]. |
| **Roving checker / inline QC** | An inline checker covering a line or 15–20 workstations [#11]. |
| **End-of-line (end-line) inspection** | Checking of completed garments at the end of the sewing line, typically 100% (referenced in #10; formal definition not fetched). |
| **Table checker** | A checker at the inspection table (term used in #1/#2 context). |
| **Traffic-light system / 7.0 (Seven-Zero) system** | Inline sampling of about 10 pcs per operator per round, with a red/yellow/green card by defectives found [#11, #12]. |
| **Final inspection / final audit (FRI)** | AQL sampling audit of a packed lot before shipment [#13]. |
| **WIP** | Work in progress: inventory started but not finished [#26]. Measured as input − output per section [#25]. |
| **Critical WIP** | Minimum WIP for the line to reach bottleneck throughput = bottleneck rate × throughput time [#27]. |
| **Bundle** | A tied group of cut pieces moved between operations. Kong et al. note bundle travel time between stations in conveyor/batch lines [#23]. A formal definition was not fetched. |
| **Line balancing** | Levelling output across operations so no WIP piles up and no operation is starved [#23]. |
| **Bottleneck** | The operation with the lowest capacity, where work accumulates [#24, #20]. |
| **LBR (line balancing rate)** | See the formula in #20. |
| **SMV / SAM** | Standard minute value / standard allowed minutes: standard time per garment or operation. Used in #20, #22, #31, #32. |
| **UPPH** | Units per person-hour [#23]. |
| **Cycle time (station)** | Time per piece at a station. CT_min is bounded below by the longest station time [#23]. |
| **Takt time** | Not found in fetched garment sources (**unverified**). Standard lean definition: available time / customer demand. |
| **Pitch time** | Not found in fetched sources (**unverified**). In garment IE usage, believed to be SMV / number of operators, i.e. the ideal per-operator share of work content. |
| **NPT / lost time / loss time** | Productive time lost while operators are present [#29]. |
| **Absenteeism (ILO)** | Unplanned leave, including sick and excluding holiday leave [#16]. |
| **Labour turnover** | Leavers / average employment × 100 over a period [#14, #15]. |
| **CPM** | Cost per minute. Variants in #31 and #32. |
| **Labour minute costing** | Fair Wear method that uses cost per minute to build living-wage floors into prices [#33]. |
